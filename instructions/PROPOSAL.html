Thesis for judges

Proposal

The industry already has portable credentials and wallet reputation. It does not have a real-time, explainable referee for when those credentials disagree across chains. ChainMind Synapse is that referee.

Novelty we can honestly claim in a 6-hour demo: conflict-aware fusion + classical ML + prompt-orchestrated explanation + on-chain commitment on two live testnets, with no IdP.

Approach

  1. Claims as first-class events on two EVMs, not as a hosted KYC dump.
  2. Standards as envelopes (VC 2.0, did:ethr), not as a 50-dependency SSI runtime.
  3. Statistical trust, not vibes. Beta reputation (Jøsang & Ismail 2002) for issuers. Subjective logic fusion (Jøsang) for claims. sklearn for contextual credibility. SHAP (Lundberg & Lee 2017) for why the model moved.
  4. LLMs as clerks, not oracles. They extract and narrate. They do not own the score. TrustJudge (2025) warns that integer LLM-as-judge scores are inconsistent — we keep the judge off the critical number.
  5. Hash commitments so a relying party can audit without trusting our API host.

Why this math, not a single softmax

A lone classifier probability cannot represent “we have no evidence” versus “we have strong conflicting evidence.” Both can look like 0.5. Subjective logic’s uncertainty mass u distinguishes ignorance from conflict after fusion. Dempster-Shafer is the ancestor; Jøsang’s operators avoid some of the classic DS conflict-normalization pathologies and map cleanly to Beta posteriors we can implement in forty lines of Python. EigenTrust is available if we later want issuer–issuer graphs; it is not required for two issuers in a demo.

What we will not over-claim

  • We do not de-anonymize people.
  • We do not replace legal identity.
  • We do not hide the operator key.
  • We do not call the commit a zk proof.
  • We do not use the holder’s government ID as evidenceURI content.

Demo story (90 seconds of plot)

Ava has an “over 18 / KYC active” claim on Ethereum. A cheaper issuer on Polygon later posts “KYC revoked / under 18.” A relying DAO cannot pick. Synapse watches both events, extracts structured signals, scores each claim, fuses them, and commits: conflict present, confidence 0.41, adult-topic unresolved, revoked-weight rising. The DAO’s front end reads the API, shows the explanation, and links the tx. Ava’s first issuer revokes nothing; the Polygon issuer is down-weighted after trust revision because it contradicts a historically reliable issuer without new evidence. If the Polygon issuer later posts a revocation of its own claim, confidence recovers.

Success, impact, and what “good” means

Demo-good is in PRD.html and the gates in REQUIREMENTS.html: two-chain conflict visible, accuracy > 75% on the hold-out, GET under 500 ms, commit under 2 minutes, hash verifies, explanation cites named features.

Product-good, if this lived past the clock: a relying party stops opening two explorers and asking a human which KYC claim to believe. Measure that as (1) share of identity queries that return a non-null verdict plus a matching stateHash, (2) drop in manual review on conflicting subjects, (3) ingest lag and commit latency SLOs from health logs. We do not claim we replace legal identity or de-anonymize anyone.

If the clock were longer

Bitstring Status List, EIP-712 signed claims, issuer EigenTrust, MiniLM evidence features, a real labeled set and model card, operator multisig, Prometheus on the structured events in ARCHITECTURE.html. We would still not wrap Passport or Trusta, still not fine-tune a judge, still not put PII on-chain. RESEARCH.html lists the steal / refuse lines.

What we are building in six hours

Not a protocol for the world. A working, inspectable slice that proves the missing layer is buildable with open libraries, open math, and two testnets. The dossier you are reading is the spec the coding agents must obey.
